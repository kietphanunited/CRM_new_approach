CEO proposal · Samsung Vietnam · Omnichannel strategy

Build an Omnichannel Hub to regain control of customers, pricing and margin

Recommendation: Samsung should build one central "brain" that connects inventory, orders, pricing and customer data across every channel, then use it to grow the revenue it controls directly (D2C + B2B) from 5% to roughly 25–30% within 3 years. This is a strategy to rebalance channels, not to abandon partners: 70%+ of revenue still flows through marketplaces and retail chains, but Samsung owns the data and gains leverage in wholesale price negotiations.

Pillar 1 · Control

Own the customer data and relationship

Today marketplaces and retail chains know who Samsung's customers are; Samsung does not. The Hub brings every transaction into one customer profile (Samsung Account).

Pillar 2 · Profit

Keep the margin retailers currently capture

Every D2C order keeps the gap between wholesale and retail price, minus delivery, marketing and operating costs. B2B brings higher margin with large, stable orders.

Pillar 3 · O2O experience

Turn the two flagships into network hubs

The Hanoi and HCMC flagships become showroom, 2-hour pickup point, setup station, and returns and service desk for orders from every channel.

A simple way to picture it: the Hub works like an airport control tower. Planes (orders) arrive from many directions: app, flagship, marketplaces, businesses. Without a tower, each runway fends for itself, with clashes and shortages. With a tower, every flight is visible on one screen and routed to the best runway (nearest warehouse, store with stock).

Loyalty that pulls customers to D2C

Wherever customers buy, they become Samsung's customers once the device is linked to a Samsung Account

Every Galaxy asks the user to sign in to a Samsung Account at first setup. TVs and appliances have SmartThings. This is a door marketplaces and retailers do not control. Loyalty turns that door into a path back to Samsung's own channels.

Roadmap

Three phases, each with a decision gate before further funding

Months 0–6 · Foundation

Connect web, app and 2 flagships to one inventory

  • Build the OMS and real-time inventory
  • Launch "Buy online, pick up in store in 2 hours"
  • Samsung Account as the single sign-in
  • Minimum price policy across all channels
Gate: ≥15% of online orders picked up in store; inventory accuracy ≥98%
Months 6–18 · Expansion

Open the B2B portal and loyalty programme

  • B2B portal: tiered quotes, credit limits, VAT invoices
  • Loyalty: points, trade-in, extended warranty
  • Ship-from-store and home delivery from flagships
  • Bring marketplace store data into customer profiles
Gate: D2C+B2B ≥15% of revenue; D2C cost to serve ≤10%
Months 18–36 · Optimisation

Use AI and invite retailers into the network

  • Channel-level demand forecasts feeding monthly S&OP
  • AI shopping assistant on app, Zalo and in store
  • Retailers fulfil D2C orders from their stock for a commission
  • Expand satellite stores if the numbers hold
Gate: 25–30% directly controlled revenue; blended margin up ≥2 pts
Risks to state plainly

The biggest risk is retailer pushback, not technology

Thế Giới Di Động, CellphoneS and the marketplaces still carry 95% of revenue today. If they see Samsung competing head-on on price, they may cut orders and push other brands. Move the "Retailer pushback" slider to 25% to see the plan turn loss-making.

RiskLevelMitigation
Channel conflict with retailersHighNever price D2C below retailers. D2C wins on experience, exclusives (colours, bundles, pre-orders) and loyalty. Invite retailers to fulfil orders for a commission in phase 3.
D2C cost to serve higher than plannedHighTrack cost per order monthly. Favour store pickup (cheaper than home delivery). Set a minimum order value for free delivery.

Lack of retail operating capabilityMediumSet up a dedicated D2C team with its own P&L. Outsource delivery, keep customer data in-house.
Marketplaces reduce visibility or raise feesMediumKeep official marketplace stores as a channel inside the Hub, don't withdraw. Use D2C data as leverage in negotiations.
